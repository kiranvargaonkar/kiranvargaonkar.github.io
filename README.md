# Hi, I’m Kiran ヾ(＾ ∇ ＾) 

Welcome to my corner of the internet. I’ve put this portfolio together to share the work I’ve been part of, the things I’m curious about, and the questions I’d like to explore next.

I’m interested in how we make decisions about shared natural resources, who benefits from them, and how communities can respond to environmental change. At the moment, I’m especially curious about:

- **Sand and shared resources** — how sand is used and extracted, who has access to it, and how the benefits and costs are shared.
- **Coastal ecosystems and biodiversity** — how coastlines and habitats change, and how we can better understand and protect them.
- **Community knowledge and climate resilience** — how local experience and inclusive decisions can help communities prepare for and recover from climate-related risks.

## What you’ll find here

There’s a mix of research and policy work, community initiatives, biodiversity projects, data visualisation, photography and writing. A few starting points are my contributions to the Climate Ireland Adaptation Network reports, JalSaksham’s community resilience work, biodiversity work at MSD Ireland, and my Wildflowers of Konkan archive.

My route here hasn’t been a straight line. I started out in mechanical engineering, moved into project management, and later completed an MSc in Business Management at Dublin City University, graduating with First Class Honours. Since then, environmental practice and climate adaptation have become increasingly important parts of my work and the questions I want to pursue.

I was also selected as one of 17 Global Citizen Leaders in the Global Citizen Scholars 2026 programme. The seed grant supports JalSaksham, a community-focused initiative around flood preparedness and recovery.

## What I’m looking for

I’m currently looking for **PhD opportunities** where I can explore environmental governance, shared natural resources, coastal ecosystems and community resilience in more depth. If you’re working on related questions, or know of a research group that might be a good fit, I’d be happy to connect.
